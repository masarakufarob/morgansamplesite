Department of Humanities

Department Overview

👤

Mr B. Ziso

Head of Department — Humanities

The Department of Humanities is one of the largest departments at the college. It comprises a range of subjects taught by competent lecturers through the blended approach where face-to-face teaching is combined with virtual learning. Among other teaching and learning approaches, team teaching, collaborative learning, tutorials, lectures and heritage tours are used by the sections in this department.

Each subject area is known as a section and is headed by a Lecturer-in-Charge. The sections can be grouped into three categories:

🗣️

Languages

ChiShona, IsiNdebele & English

🌍

Social Sciences

Family, Religion & Moral Education

🏫

Contemporaries

Health & Life Skills, NASS

The department prepares pre-service teachers for critical citizenship, understanding of cultures and indigenous knowledge systems of the people of Zimbabwe and offers character education. It holds the greatest responsibility in developing students' soft skills such as communication abilities, language skills, emotional habits, empathy, time management, collaboration, teamwork and leadership traits.

Student teachers are empowered with requisite 21st century competencies for effective facilitation of learning at primary school level. The department has given birth to a number of clubs including Chinese, Debate and Public Speaking, Peer Education, Scripture Union, First Aid and the Environmental Education Club.

Academic Sections

Department Sections

Each section within the Department of Humanities is headed by a Lecturer-in-Charge and offers specific areas of study within the teacher education curriculum.

Indigenous Languages

Bazi ReChiShona

Lecturer-in-Charge: Mrs Sibanda

Bazi iri rinodzidzisa ChiShona — mutauro waamai. Vadzidzi vanodzidza tsika namagariro. Rinodzidzisa zvidzidzwa zviviri: ChiShona Chamadzidzisiro neChiShona Choudzamu.

ChiShona Chamadzidzisiro — Chinodzidzisa mudzidzi kuti ave neruzivo rwekudzidzisa ChiShona muzvikoro.

ChiShona Choudzamu — Chinobata zvidzidzwa zvetsika nemagariro, mutauro, nhetembo, nganonyorwa nemitambo.

Indigenous Languages

Ugatsha LweSiNdebele

Coordinator: Dr T. Ndlovu

Established in response to students from Ndebele-speaking regions, this section provides instruction in IsiNdebele for teaching and communication. Currently all students are offered the opportunity to learn IsiNdebele to widen their linguistic competence.

For students pursuing IsiNdebele as a teaching subject, the programme prepares them to effectively teach at the primary school level.

Languages

English

Lecturer-in-Charge: Mrs J. Hungwe

Comprises three areas: English Professional Studies Syllabus B (PSB), English Main Subject and Communication Skills. PSB and Communication Skills are compulsory to all students; English Main Subject is for those who choose to specialise.

PSB exposes student teachers to methods of teaching listening, speaking, reading and writing. The Main Subject covers Literature in English, Language and Linguistics. Communication Skills develops effective communication habits for the global village.

Social Sciences

Family, Religion & Moral Education (FAREME)

Lecturer-in-Charge: Dr Tatira

A Social Science section comprising PSB and Main Subject areas. PSB develops student teachers who can effectively teach family dynamics, religion and moral issues to primary school learners, fostering appreciation of diverse beliefs.

The Main Subject explores World Religions, beliefs, practices and contemporary religious issues, equipping teachers for multi-faith communities.

Social Sciences

Heritage-Social Studies (H-SS)

Lecturer-in-Charge: Mr T. Dzumbunu

PSB area prepares all student teachers to effectively teach Heritage-Social Studies at primary level — moulding human character through identity, norms, values, patriotism and heritage resources. Empowers teachers to create spaces where learners engage with climate change, local cultures and children's rights.

The Main Subject provides a platform to explore civil, social, economic, political and developmental concerns at local and international levels.

Contemporaries

Health and Life Skills (HLS)

Lecturer-in-Charge: Pastor Maviko

A compulsory subject focusing on development of knowledge, attitudes, values and skills to positively influence students' ability to adopt safer behaviours. Uses participatory methods: Focus Group Discussions, Workshops, Role Plays, Drama, Lectures.

Activities include life skills workshops for new intakes, peer educator workshops, TP deployment preparation, outreach programmes and guidance and counselling services.

Contemporaries

National & Strategic Studies (NASS)

Lecturer-in-Charge: Mr Masungo

A compulsory subject for all students. Develops awareness of the historical context informing national and international discourse on Zimbabwe. Instills loyalty, patriotism and positive national values.

Topics: History of Zimbabwe, National and International Relations, Civic Education, Contemporary Issues.

Student Activities

Department Clubs

Student teachers have opportunities to explore further what they learn in formal spaces into independent areas of interest through clubs. The Department of Humanities has given birth to several vibrant clubs. Club content and student representatives are to be developed further by students in their respective clubs.

🇨🇳

Chinese Club

Promotes Chinese language and cultural exchange. Connected to the college's Diploma in Chinese Language offering.

🎤

Debate & Public Speaking

Develops critical thinking, argumentation and public speaking skills essential for future educators.

🤝

Peer Educators' Club

Student peer educators trained to support fellow students on health, life skills and wellness topics.

✝️

Scripture Union

A faith-based club fostering spiritual growth and moral values aligned with the college's ethos.

🩺

First Aid Club

Equips student teachers with first aid knowledge and practical emergency response skills.

🌱

Environmental Education Club

Promotes environmental awareness, sustainability and heritage-based ecological responsibility.

📝 Note to students: Club pages are to be developed by students in their respective clubs. Please submit your club information, student representatives, activities and photos to the Department of Humanities.
